What this is

A fragrance consultation built around Les Extraits

Read this once. You will not need it again.

How it works

You ask the client three questions and tap what you hear. From those answers, this tool composes a selection of three Extraits out of the four, ranked, and gives you the reason to present each one. Ten to fifteen minutes, seated with your client.

Why we do it this way

An Extrait is not sold by explaining its price. It is sold by the attention that goes into it. A client who has been listened to for fifteen minutes is not holding the same object as a client who was handed a blotter across a counter.

And the attention is already in the bottle. Each Extrait took twice as long to develop as a standard Parfums de Marly creation, pushed and refined until nothing could be improved. Time itself is an ingredient. The consultation simply gives the client the same thing the Maison gave the fragrance.

What you should have at the end

A client who recognises themselves in the selection, who has held the bottle and seen the engraved cap, and who understands why these three and not the others. Whether they buy today or come back for it.

One rule

The device stays in your hands, never the client's. It carries the steps so that you can keep your eyes on them.

Before you begin

Set the table, then start when you are with the client

Check everything below is in place. Press start once you are standing with the client, not before.

Everything below must be in place
  • A tray within reach
  • Gloves in your pocket
  • Two blotters per fragrance
  • A seat for the client, and one for you — you sit with them
  • Still water, sparkling, coffee or tea if your location allows
  • A set of the four Extraits kept for consultations, never half-empty bottles
Open with this

« I'd like us to take ten to fifteen minutes together, so I can introduce you to the most exclusive expression of Parfums de Marly — Les Extraits collection. »

« Please, take a seat. I'll guide you through a fragrance consultation, and by the end we'll have found together the Extrait that suits you best. »

Why this way: the client did not walk in looking for an Extrait. You are not serving a request — you are offering a discovery. The seat comes inside the offer, not before it.

Question 1 of 3

« What do you know about Parfums de Marly? »

Ask it, then tap what you heard. This sets how much you tell them next — it does not choose a fragrance.

Before moving on

Give them this much, no more

Say it in your own words. Stop when it lands — the rest of the house comes later, if they ask.

Say this

Question 2 of 3

« What matters most to you in a fragrance? »

Then say this

« Let me give you three different angles on that. I'll read them to you, and you stop me on anything that sounds like you — as many as you like, from any of the three. There is no wrong answer. »

Read every option aloud. The client picks across all three angles, not one angle and not one answer. The more they stop you, the sharper the selection.

Nothing selected yet

Question 3 of 3

« And what is your fragrancing ritual? »

Read the four aloud and let the client recognise themselves. The answer barely matters — what it opens does. Tapping one takes you to the composition.

Now stand up

« Thank you — that's very helpful. Let me compose a selection for you. » Leave, fetch the tray, place these three on it in this order, come back. Put the gloves on in front of the client, at the moment you pick up the bottle.

Offer a blind discovery

« May I ask you to close your eyes? I'd like you to meet these without seeing the bottle or the colour of the juice — nothing to influence you but the scent itself. » Most clients say yes, and it changes the whole moment. If they'd rather not, carry on as normal.

While they smell

Two blotters — one for the client, one for you. Say nothing until they have reacted. Name the fragrance after, never before. Flat reaction? Move on: « I can see this one isn't speaking to you — let me show you the second. »

Then hand it over

Place the bottle in the client's hands — the weight, the glass, the engraving. Then turn it and show the cap: every Extrait carries its own guilloché motif drawn from the Château de Marly.

If an objection comes up

« Why is it more expensive? »

Bergamot, the most expensive citrus in perfumery. Geranium Bourbon, the rarest floral geranium there is. Patchouli Heart, a purified fraction. Damascena rose oil, costlier to produce than the absolute. Blackcurrant buds: 25 kg for a single kilo. « You're not buying a fragrance. You're buying French excellence in a collector's object. »

« It doesn't last on me. »

Intensity varies from skin to skin — it isn't a quality issue. Always try it on skin, never only on a blotter. « After a while it's normal not to smell your own fragrance any more. Ask around you — the people near you still can. »

« It doesn't smell like Marly. »

By design. Les Extraits marks a further evolution of the House — a more expressive, more distinctive territory. « For those who want to stand out. »

« Valero isn't really niche. »

Intentional. In niche perfumery a luminous, clean Extrait is the rarest thing there is — that's the audacity. Underneath the clarity: Ambrox Super, Geranium Bourbon, an exceptional concentration of bergamot. « Concentration as refinement, not heaviness. »

« The bottle looks plain. »

Handcrafted in France. 50% recycled premium transparent glass, guilloché-engraved cap in 75% recycled Zamak, reusable box with a silver guilloché plaque. « Hold it. Feel the difference. »

However it ends

Walk the client to the door. Thank them for the time they gave you, and tell them what they can come back for.
